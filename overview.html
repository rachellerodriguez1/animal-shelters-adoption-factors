Project overview

What helps animals get adopted?

When welcoming a new pet into your home, having clear information such as breed, background, sex, fertility status, and health condition is essential. These factors shape a pet's energy level, training needs, and long-term care. Knowing this data ensures a seamless match between your lifestyle and your new companion's unique needs. Shelter data from Best Friends and the University of Florida suggests that adoption outcomes are influenced most strongly by health and fertility status, while breed and sex matter to a lesser degree depending on the shelter and animal type.

Key findings

  • Health is the leading predictor. Pets with fewer medical barriers usually find homes faster.
  • Sterilization matters. Spayed and neutered animals are often easier to adopt because they are more immediately ready for placement.
  • Breed and sex influence demand. These can shift depending on local adoption preference, shelter mix, and the dog/cat population.

Why the project matters

  • Adoption speed is closely connected to readiness for home life.
  • Medical stability and clear care information reduce uncertainty for adopters.
  • Data helps shelters allocate staff time, foster capacity, and treatment resources more effectively.
